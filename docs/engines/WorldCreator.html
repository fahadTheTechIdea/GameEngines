WorldCreator

A reusable, engine-independent world metadata creator: expand a game's catalog archetypes and a recipe of scope, space and constraints into a deterministic world document — hierarchy, connections, regions, placement, networks, routes, interiors, services, progression, layers, chunks and ground cover.

net8.0 metadata only deterministic digests no renderer required
Current scope. The generated deliverable is metadata: JSON and optional JSON/SVG/plan export. It does not by itself prove collision, navigation, gameplay, presentation or art approval. A later renderer, Godot adapter or GameArts pipeline is an optional downstream consumer.

Purpose

WorldCreator turns reusable game-defined content into a world. A catalog declares archetypes (a house, a road, a tree, a district) with parts, connection rules and physical footprints. A recipe selects a root archetype, a scale, a coordinate space and the constraints for one world. The generator expands programs into nodes and connections, then plans placement, access, networks, routes and the rest, producing a reproducible document.

Metadata-first

No image, biome raster, asset registry, renderer or game engine is needed. Coordinates are optional; when requested they come from declared layout grammars and constraints.

Deterministic

Recipe and catalog are hashed (SHA-256), stages carry dependency identities, and the same inputs produce the same document — verified by a complete source replay.

Purposeful placement

Every node has an archetype, purpose, parent and region; placement is constrained. No arbitrary scattering or vacant-cell fallback.

Generation pipeline

  1. Validate recipe and catalog: schema version, ids, bounds, archetype graph (depth ≤ 32, no cycles), parts, rules and layer memberships.
  2. Content expansion — expand archetype parts into nodes, connection rules into connections, apply variations and capacity definitions.
  3. Terrain — evaluate the declared surface, drainage, water and wind/moisture fields.
  4. Regions — choose region geometry from the request or region demand; inherit a source habitat when the recipe is population-derived.
  5. Allocation — local layout and/or physical placement; feasibility search with no-goods; optional adaptation; final positions.
  6. Access — approaches, entrances, pedestrian reachability and site interfaces.
  7. Environment and interiors — suitability, interior space/walls and travel surface.
  8. Networks and routes — plan utility/road/rail/pedestrian/water graphs, then routes with costs and budgets.
  9. Corridors and services — corridor geometry and service coverage/relations.
  10. Context, progression, layers — habitat context, technology gates, named layers.
  11. Chunks, ground cover, population coordination — chunk packages, ground-cover materials and validated population inheritance.
  12. Accounting and validation — physical ground-use coverage, dependency identities, result validation.

Each stage is a named execution with input and dependency fingerprints. MetadataExecutionCache memoises expensive network/route/placement work within one run; a changed dependency refuses reuse (METADATA_GENERATION_DEPENDENCY_CHANGED_DURING_RUN).

The world document

WorldMetadataDocument (schema 2) carries:

  • Identity: id, game profile, scale, coverage, remaining scopes, space and the recipe/catalog SHA-256 digests.
  • Nodes (id, archetype, kind, parent, purpose, properties, optional position, structure/variation keys) and Connections (kind, from/to, bidirectionality, source rule, layer).
  • Plans: layers, structures, map geometry, facilities, objects, areas, surface, routes, networks, environment, placement, footprints, access, capacity, services, context, corridors, regions, local layout, adaptation, progression, variation, chunks, topology, interiors, terrain demand, program mix/composition, drainage, water, wind/moisture, population coordination, region demand and ground cover.

No node is placed without a defined archetype and purpose. Optional map geometry (boxes, roads, polygons) is derived from the plans, not invented by a renderer.

Constraints and validation

  • Root archetype must exist; every part references a known archetype; archetype cycles and depth beyond 32 are refused.
  • Budgets: ≤ 100,000 nodes, ≤ 500,000 connections, ≤ 50,000 additional connections, ≤ 256 parameters, ≤ 8 axes, ≤ 1,000 archetypes, ≤ 10,000 parts.
  • Connection rules must be complete: a Chain has no target; every other pattern names a valid part.
  • Layers must not conflict with member layers; route/network kinds cannot collide; a route budget needs route rules.
  • Placement requires a surface and environment; footprints require a physical allocation definition; a fragmentary recipe is refused rather than silently degraded.
  • Determinism: the same recipe and catalog produce the same document; verify-metadata replays the complete source and compares.

Population, chunks and ground cover

  • populationSource derives a world from a source world's program mix and adapted layouts. The consumer may inherit the source's qualified habitat but cannot choose another region after counts were derived from it.
  • chunks publishes spatial chunks for streaming/selection, with a geometry index and package reader.
  • groundCover assigns surfaces from domains/patches/exclusions; unresolved ground is reported, not silently covered.
  • MetadataPhysicalAccounting checks required ground use and capacity; measurements distinguish measured, not applicable and unmeasured.

Approved mockup capture

When a world is derived from an approved GameArts mockup, the capture path records what was observed, inferred, authored or unknown, with shape (point/polyline/polygon), junction kind, review state and coverage (partial or complete).

  • capture-schema prints the capture document schema.
  • review-capture validates a capture against its schema and the trusted asset root.
  • compile-capture compiles a reviewed capture into a geometry draft.
  • GameArts gates (GameArtsProductionGate, GameArtsAtlasValidator, GameArtsSemanticWorldPipeline) require approved assets/atlases and motion metadata before production use. A scene skeleton is not a visually complete world.

Export and structure pipeline

WorldDocumentExporter writes world JSON and, with a plan renderer, an SVG/plan view. IWorldExporter is the boundary. CoordinatedSettlementWorldBuilder composes settlement-scale content with the same planners.

structure-pipeline runs the structure-first stages: metadata, reconcile, ground, scene, production-inventory, production-bind, native-static, tile-topology.

Limits and honest status

  • The procedural city generator and its network/district/parcel orchestration were removed; they are not current capabilities and must not be restored as the foundation.
  • Metadata completeness is not gameplay completeness. A complete declared scope does not prove collision, pathfinding, balance or a playable game.
  • Passing geometry checks is not owner design approval, production art or functional Godot acceptance.
  • Optional realism simulations (traffic engineering, photometric coverage, drainage physics) are opt-in, never default acceptance gates.
