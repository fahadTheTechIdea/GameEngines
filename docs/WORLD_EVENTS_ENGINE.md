Decision: **the world-events layer owns what nobody owns, owns no clock, and is only ever read through bridges.** It is a pure function of a document, a seed, the steps it was given (each with its season) and the calls it answered. Every random number is the counter-based `EngineCommon.DeterministicRoll` of (seed, stream, step) or of a draw counter that is part of the state, so a replay or a checkpoint resume is exact. No float, no wall clock, no generator with a state (a Roslyn guard fails on any).

## 2. Research (searched 2026-10-29)

Reached (page or search result read, claims below are from them):

- **RimWorld storytellers** (rimworldwiki.com/wiki/AI_Storytellers, fetched): Cassandra follows "a classic increasing curve of challenge and tension" with breathing room; Phoebe gives "lots of time between disasters"; Randy generates events "with no pacing curve". Inputs to timing: wealth, colonist count, recent deaths, time since the last major event. Difficulty is separate from the storyteller (threat scale 10% to 220%). The wiki gives no formulas, rest lengths or weights.
- **Gutenberg-Richter law** (Wikipedia, fetched): log10 N = a - bM; b close to 1, between 0.5 and 2; a one-unit fall in magnitude multiplies the count by 10^b.
- **Compartmental models** (Wikipedia, first 100,000 characters): SIR equations, R0 = beta/gamma, the final-size relation s_inf = s_0 exp(-R0 (r_inf - r_0)). SEIR is mentioned only as the latent-period variant; its equations were not in the part read (the standard form, E to I at rate 1/incubation, is used and tested against an integration of the same equations).
- **Koeppen classification** (Wikipedia, first 100,000 characters): five groups A to E; Cfb is temperate, no dry season, warmest month below 22 degrees C. The document's `koppen` field is a label for people, not an input.
- **Markov weather generators** (search results only): two-state first-order chains for rainfall occurrence (Gabriel and Neumann 1962), Richardson 1981 for seasonality, an amounts model for wet days, Wilks and Wilby 1999. Stated weakness: such chains underestimate the variance of spell lengths and extreme dry spells.
- **Left 4 Dead director** (search results only): a finite state machine that adds pressure and eases off, tracking each survivor's perceived stress; a designer's description of slow-quiet-then-chaos. The build-up / sustain / peak / fade / relax phases were **not** found in a source read; the phase machine here is this engine's own, named after the idea.

Not reached (no claim is made from them): Dwarf Fortress and Crusader Kings event systems, Oregon Trail, Civilization disasters, Frostpunk, Kermack and McKendrick 1927 itself (the final-size result is checked from the Wikipedia statement), integer-discretisation literature for Poisson processes (the error analysis in section 4 is this document's own arithmetic), the Wikipedia pages for SIR and AI director returned 404 at the URLs tried.

## 3. Adopt / adapt / avoid

| Source | Adopt | Adapt | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| RimWorld | A storyteller is a profile separate from the difficulty; events come in categories; wealth-independent pacing | Storyteller = pacing parameters + tier shares; difficulty = four scale factors; no wealth input (the host can feed one as a fact) | Hidden formulas: every number is in the document and explained by `explain` |
| Left 4 Dead | Pressure builds, peaks, then rests | Stress with decay; phases Build, Peak (held), Relax; bad things only in Build | Per-player stress: one world stress |
| Markov weather | Persistence plus seasonal climatology | Lazy resampling: stay with probability p, else draw again from the whole distribution, so the long-run share of each weather equals its weight whatever p. A regime layer (fronts) tilts the weights | Assuming persistence changes climate: it only changes spell length |
| SIR/SEIR | Compartments, force of infection, R0 | Whole people, stochastic rounding of each flow, travel as mixing along links, interventions as multipliers | Stochastic extinction models of N=1: expected flows with unbiased integer rounding |
| Gutenberg-Richter | Rarer when worse | Severity ladder with tail probabilities `tailPpm` (the first rung is certain) | A continuous magnitude: rungs are named, the tail table is the discretisation |
| Poisson hazards | Rate per step | Bernoulli per step: error is the chance of two onsets in one step, about half the square of the rate | Rates above 0.1 per step (not refused; documented) |

## 4. Contract

**Units, all whole numbers.** Temperature in hundredths of a degree C; precipitation in tenths of a millimetre; wind in tenths of a metre a second; shares per mille; probabilities in ppm; time in steps (a day is `stepsPerDay` of them, normally one).

**Time and seasons (the calendar contract).** `WorldRuntime.Advance(toStep, seasonAt, hostFacts)` lives the steps after `Step` in order. The season of each step is given by the caller: the calendar bridge returns the calendar's own season of the day (leap years and moved season starts included); a game with no calendar uses the document's cycle. Everything is checked before the first step runs.

**Weather.** Per region and step: the regime (Markov, persistence, per-season frequency, a bias per weather) tilts the zone-season weights; the weather stays with the zone's persistence or is redrawn. Temperature = zone-season mean + AR(1) anomaly (stationary spread = sigma: the innovation is scaled by `isqrt(1e6 - phi^2)`) + the weather's offset. Noise is the sum of four 16-bit parts of one roll centred (a bell of standard deviation 577 before scaling, cut off at 3.5 sigma: a stated error). Rain is the weather's amount times a uniform 0.4 to 1.6, with a heavy-tail mixture (share and factor in the hydrology). Hydrology: snowpack below freezing, melt by the degree, a soil bucket with evaporation by the degree, runoff to a river store that drains by a share; all in whole tenths of a millimetre, rounded down.

**Hazards.** Onset chance per step = rate x season share x region exposure x difficulty (each per mille), a Bernoulli trial; a condition over region facts (`river gt 600`) gates it; paced hazards wait for the director to be building; exclusions are symmetric; a cooldown follows the end. Severity from the ladder's tails (`tailPpm`, strictly falling). Life: onset (rising), peak (drawn length), recovery (falling), then gone; a peak may escalate a rung per step with `escalatePpm`. Reach: linked regions within `hops` get the same rung at power `falloff^hops`; shocks scale by the power. Modifiers (visibility, mobility, labour...) are the base plus weather, hazards (scaled by intensity), diseases (scaled by prevalence up to a line) and timed card effects, clamped to their range.

**Disease.** Per region and disease, from the compartments at the start of the step: infections = S x I_eff x beta / N (I_eff adds the neighbours' infectious people times the link flow and both regions' travel restriction); E to I after the incubation (SEIR); I leaves at 1/infectious scaled by treatment, a share dies; recovered lose immunity at 1/waning; vaccination moves S to R. Every flow is a quotient taken in 128 bits and rounded **stochastically** (up with exactly the probability of the fraction, drawn from the stream), so the expected flow is the model's and the people add up (checked after every step). The error against an Euler integration of the same equations is rounding only (section 10). Outbreak states (introduced, outbreak at a prevalence line, contained at half of it, ended) announce themselves once; interventions start by hand or by an automatic line with the same half-line hysteresis.

**Director and deck.** Stress 0 to 1000 decays by a share a step; bad things add (scaled by the difficulty), good cards relieve it. Phases: Build until stress reaches the peak line, Peak for a hold, Relax until stress is below the relax line and the rest has lasted. After the minimum gap a card is drawn with a chance rising to certainty at the maximum gap; weights are card weight x tier share x (difficulty for bad cards); bad cards only while building; cooldowns, exclusion groups (held for a duration), conditions over region or world facts, region choice among those where it holds, chains (delay, probability, own condition checked when due), timed modifiers, bounded agenda. A storyteller whose peak line is above 1000 is the random one.

**Output.** One ordered list of changes per call (`WorldChange`), turned into events by the component: season, regime, weather, hazard (started, escalated, receding, ended), outbreak, card, director, intervention and **effects** (`signal` and `shock`) for other layers.

**Bounds.** 1,024 regions, 8,192 links, 64 hazards (8 rungs), 32 diseases, 128 interventions, 512 cards, 16 storytellers; active hazards, timed modifiers and agenda have document limits; an owed-event queue with back-pressure and at most 366 steps per Update.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

W1 deterministic: same document and seed, same state digest; any split of a run (save, jump, resume) equals the straight run. W2 climatology: long-run weather shares equal the weights for any persistence; persistence lengthens spells. W3 temperature follows zone and season with the stated spread. W4 water rules hold (bucket bounds, running sums). W5 hazard onset frequency equals the rate where eligible; severity frequencies equal the tails. W6 hazard life exact on the steps (intensity table). W7 exclusion, cooldown, season, condition, exposure, difficulty, capacity, pacing each shown. W8 people conserved after every step for any seed. W9 the compartment model agrees with an integration of the same equations; final size of R0 = 2 is 0.797 within 0.03. W10 interventions, travel, outbreak states, burden. W11 director phases, spacing, tiers, groups, chains, timed modifiers, bounds. W12 save: ranges before digest, more than 40 forgeries refused as states. W13 component: requests answered in order, every request answered, back-pressure, quiet checkpoint, replay exact from a checkpoint for seeded random request streams. W14 every bridge has a proof. W15 100 regions x 20 hazards x 20 diseases x one year within budget. W16 source guards: no clock, float, file access, swallowed catch, throwing default arm or `?? throw`; six channel registries, one owner.

## 8. Limits stated up front

Temperature jumps at a season boundary by the difference of the means (add an early/late season to smooth it). Diseases are independent models over the same people (no comorbidity; the region's population fact falls by all deaths). The noise has a short tail. A Bernoulli onset is not a Poisson count (error below 0.5% for rates under 0.01 a step). Rain amounts are not gamma. The director's phases are this engine's own, not copied from a published source. The per-hazard `when` is evaluated after a successful rate roll, which does not change the distribution. A clock beyond the calendar's range is published as a clock fault and the replay of that fault reports a different code.

### 11.1 Research (fetched 2026-10-29)

Wikipedia, Hawkes process (fetched again in this pass; the fetch tool summarises with a small model, I did not see raw text): the branching ratio is the integral of the kernel, "the average number of daughters of each arrival"; descendants are finite with probability 1 when it is 1 or less and each arrival has a positive chance of infinitely many when it is above 1; arrivals viewed as descendants form a Galton-Watson branching process. The page gives **no** exponential kernel, recursive update, total-progeny formula, index of dispersion or ETAS. So the recursive form, the cluster size 1 / (1 - n) and the dispersion 1 / (1 - n)^2 for long windows are this document's own derivations (standard branching-process arithmetic), checked by test (11.5). The review (section 3 W1, 7) carries the earlier reading (Gutenberg-Richter, Markov weather generators) and the reasons Perlin noise and wave function collapse were rejected for weather. Not reached: the Hawkes (1971) and Hawkes and Oakes (1974) papers, ETAS papers, spatial weather generators beyond the review's snippets.

### 11.2 Hawkes self-excitation (recursive integer form)

Every hazard declares `excite: { jumpPpm, decayPermille, capPpm }` (required; a hazard that does not excite itself says `jumpPpm 0, capPpm 0`). Per (hazard, region) the state is one whole number, the excitation E in millionths of an onset per step (saved; O(1) per pair, no event list). Each step: `E = E x (1000 - decayPermille) / 1000` (rounded down, so E reaches 0 and a cluster ends), then the chance of an onset is `min(1,000,000, own rate + E)`, rolled with the same counter-based roll as before; each **origin** onset (the world's own, a card's, a script's `BeginHazard`) then adds `jumpPpm`, up to `capPpm`. A hazard that only spread into a region adds nothing there (otherwise one onset would cause `1 + reach` children and the bound below would be wrong). The fade is done before the going / resting / excluded / not-exposed checks, so it falls on every step.

**Branching ratio** n = jump x (1000 - decay) / (1000 x decay), shown as `BranchingPpm` (millionths, rounded down): an onset adds jump x (1 - d)^k to the chance k steps later, summed over k >= 1. **Stationarity bound:** the reader refuses a hazard whose exact ratio is not below 1 (`jump x (1000 - decay) < 1,000,000 x decay`, no rounding) with `WE_NONSTATIONARY`; a cap below the jump, or a cap without a jump, is `WE_HAZARD`. Below 1 a cluster has a mean of 1 / (1 - n) onsets. Every blocking rule (an onset waits for the hazard to end and its cooldown; exclusions, conditions, pacing, capacity) only lowers the realised rate, so the bound is safe. **Resting step:** in this engine an onset is a hazard that lives, so the step after one is always a resting step (the hazard is removed at the start of it and the cooldown counts from there); the first, strongest term of the sum cannot cause an onset. The ratio that acts for a one-step hazard is jump x (1 - d)^2 / d (d = decay / 1000); for hazards that live longer the loss is larger. The stated n is the safe upper bound, not the realised ratio.

**Explain:** `explain ... hazard` prints `chance a step in <season>: C ppm (...)` and, for an exciting hazard, `= own rate B + excitation E ppm (each onset here adds J, the excitation falls by D per mille a step and never passes C)` and `branching ratio N ppm: ... a cluster is about X onsets in all`.

### 11.3 Weather correlated through the region graph

Every zone declares `spill` (per mille, 0 to 900, required). The region graph is built once in `WorldSet.Graph` by `EngineCommon.Graph` (`GraphBuilder`, `CsrGraph`): a link is two edges weighted by its flow. Disease mixing and weather read its edges, and `Spread` uses the shared `Bfs.Run` (the private BFS is gone; powers per distance are the same, checked against a test-side BFS and by the format-1 equivalence hashes in 11.5). When a region redraws its weather, each weather's weight (zone frequency x regime bias) is multiplied by the tilt `1000 + spill x (the share of the linked regions that have that weather - its share in the untilted weights) / 1000`, all per mille, truncated toward zero. A neighbourhood with the zone's own climate tilts nothing; a wetter-than-usual one makes rain likelier; a spill of 900 leaves every tilt at least 100/1000, so a weather the zone can draw is never tilted to nothing and one it cannot stays impossible. Every region reads its neighbours' weather **of the step before** (a snapshot), so the order of regions cannot show (tested by reversing the document's region and link order). A zone with spill 0, or a region with no link, draws exactly as in format 1. `explain ... weather <region>` prints the neighbours' weather and the untilted and tilted shares.

What this replaces and what it does not: the **global regime stays** (it is the continental front; its facts, changes and bridges are unchanged) and each region still rolls from its own counter-based stream, but a region's redraw is now correlated with its neighbours' weather. The review asked for regional correlation; removing the global regime was not done (it would change the regime facts and four bridges) and is not needed for correlation. The AR(1) temperature anomaly is still independent per region.

**Stated error.** The tilt is mean-neutral only to first order. With weights 2:1, two linked regions, persistence 500, the long-run share of the minority (wet) weather at spill 0, 300, 600, 900 is 0.3333, 0.3369, 0.3514, 0.3889 by the oracle (measured 0.3338, 0.3360, 0.3511, 0.3905): the climate drifts toward the minority weather as spill grows. The W2 test (long-run shares equal the weights for any persistence) holds for spill 0; with spill the climate is the oracle's, not the zone's weights.

### 11.5 Validation (tests with independent oracles)

- **Recursion, step for step** (4 parameter sets, 4,000 steps each): a test-side recursion with the engine's counter-based roll reproduces every onset and every value of E, including the cap and the saturation of the chance at certainty.
- **Cluster size** (12,000 scripted onsets, rate 0, jump 30,000 ppm, decay 100 per mille; stated n 0.270): mean cluster **1.3135 +- 0.0065** against the textbook 1 / (1 - 0.270) = 1.3699 and against 1.3210 with the resting step counted; empirical branching 1 - 1/mean = 0.2387 against 0.243 acting and 0.270 stated. A second run (jump 50,000, stated 0.45, acting 0.405): 1.6629 +- 0.0118 against 1.6807 (acting) and 1.8182 (textbook). The textbook figure is an upper bound that the resting step lowers; the lowered figure is what the engine achieves, within about 1% (the test allows 2%).
- **Clustering of counts** (windows of 200 steps, rate 5,000 ppm): index of dispersion 0.979 without excitation (Bernoulli: 1 - rate) and **1.574** with it, against 1.745 from 1 / (1 - n)^2 with the acting n (the long-window formula overstates for windows holding about 1.3 events; the test band is 0.85 to 1.2 of the formula); the mean count rises by 1.314, the cluster size.
- **Weather**: the stationary distribution of the joint chain of two linked regions (power iteration in floating point) against 400,000 engine steps: agreement 0.5556 / 0.5946 / 0.6343 / 0.6794 by the oracle against 0.5554 / 0.5949 / 0.6343 / 0.6756 measured at spill 0 / 300 / 600 / 900. A region whose three neighbours never change settles on 1.4/2.6 (neighbours wet) and 0.8/3.2 (neighbours dry) as the closed form says; the tilt of one redraw is also worked by hand.
- **SIR/SEIR** still matches the independent floating-point integration (DiseaseTests unchanged and passing; the disease code now reads its neighbours from the shared graph).
- **Format-1 equivalence**: with excitation off, spill 0 (and the valley's three sample changes undone) the engine gives the same changes and end state as the pass-38 engine, by SHA-256 over 800 steps of the small document, 400 certain-hazard steps and 730 steps of the valley (the hashes were recorded from the old engine before the change).

### 11.6 Measurements (Release, 100 regions x 20 hazards x 20 diseases, a year of daily steps, three runs, `docs/measurements/worldevents-scale-2026-10-29-pass42.csv`)

### 12.2 The command

```
WorldEventsEngine.Cli simulate <world.json> <scenario.json> [<steps>] --seeds <n> [--from-seed <s>] [--days <d>] [--metrics a,b,...] [--rows]
```

The same scenario (host facts, scripted calls) is lived once per seed `s, s+1, ..., s+n-1`, headless, with only the document's top-level `seed` replaced (`SeedDocument.WithSeed`; every other part of the document is carried over). The horizon is the positional number (steps here, periods for the Economy, days for Relationship) **or** `--days` (this tool: days times steps-per-day; the Economy refuses days that are not whole periods), exactly one. The run loop is not copied: `Simulate` and the sweep both call one private `Live` method in each tool, so the sweep cannot drift from the single run (the single-run output is byte-identical to before; a test starts a one-seed sweep at the document's own seed and compares its metrics to the values that run prints).

Per seed an engine supplies a function `seed -> named integer metrics`; `SeedSweep` (shared) runs the seeds **one after the other in seed order** (nothing is parallel, so the output cannot depend on scheduling), requires the same metric names from every seed (`BAL_METRICS_DIFFER`), keeps the metrics asked for, and writes:

```
balance <engine> <set id> digest <document digest> seeds <n> from <s> horizon <h> <unit>
metric <name> n <n> min <v> p10 <v> p50 <v> p90 <v> p99 <v> max <v> mean <int.ddd> positive <seeds with v > 0> median95 <lo>..<hi>|n/a [zero-upper95-ppm <v>]
...                      (ordinal name order)
sample <name> <seed> <value>          (only with --rows: by name, then seed)
sweep-digest <SHA-256 of every line above>
```

Lines end with a line feed. There is no floating point in the reducer: percentiles are nearest-rank (rank `ceil(p n / 100)` of the sorted values), the mean is the exact rational `sum / n` printed to three decimals and rounded half up (toward plus infinity: `floor((2000 sum + n) / 2n)` in thousandths), `median95` is the sign-test interval of 12.1, `positive` is a count. Exit codes: 0 done; 1 the document is refused (its own code, printed to the output as ever); 2 a bad sweep command line (`refused <code>: <why>` on the error stream) or a scenario or file problem.

| Code | Raised when |
|---|---|
| `BAL_SEEDS_MISSING` | `--seeds` is absent in the reducer's parser (the tools only enter the sweep when `--seeds` is present; without it they run the ordinary single `simulate`) |
| `BAL_SEEDS_RANGE` | `--seeds` is outside 1 to 100,000 |
| `BAL_FROM_SEED_RANGE` | `--from-seed` is outside 0 to 9,000,000,000,000,000, or the last seed would be above it |
| `BAL_DAYS_RANGE` | `--days` is outside 1 to 1,000,000, or (Economy) not a whole number of periods |
| `BAL_HORIZON_MISSING`, `BAL_HORIZON_TWICE` | neither or both of the positional horizon and `--days`, or two positional numbers |
| `BAL_OPTION_UNKNOWN`, `BAL_OPTION_TWICE`, `BAL_OPTION_VALUE` | an option the sweep does not have (`--save`, `--verbose`); an option given twice; an option without a value, a value that is not a whole number, or a horizon outside the tool's bound |
| `BAL_METRIC_UNKNOWN`, `BAL_METRIC_NAME` | a `--metrics` name the engine does not report (the message lists them all); a name that is empty, has a space or is repeated |

### 12.4 The offline tail tool and the floating-point boundary

```
EngineCommon.TailTool propose <sweep.txt> <metric> --levels a,b,c,... [--threshold-percent P (default 90)] [--min-exceedances K (default 30)]
```

Input: the `sample` rows of a sweep written with `--rows` (other lines are ignored). It proposes, for each level, the `tailPpm` of a hazard ladder rung: **the chance that a value is at least that level, in parts per million**, which is what `RungDef.TailPpm` carries ("the chance an onset is at least this bad"; the first rung is `1000000` by the document rule and is forced, and the rungs must fall strictly). Method: the threshold `u` is the nearest-rank percentile `P`; the values above it must number at least `K` (else `TAIL_FEW_EXCEEDANCES`: run more seeds); a generalized Pareto distribution is fitted to the excesses by probability-weighted moments, `a0 = mean(y)`, `a1 = mean((1 - p(i)) y(i))` with `p(i) = (i - 0.35) / k`, `xi = 2 - a0 / (a0 - 2 a1)`, `sigma = 2 a0 a1 / (a0 - 2 a1)`; a level at or under `u` is counted from the sample, a level above is `(k / n) (1 + xi t / sigma)^(-1/xi)` with `t = level - 1/2 - u` (the values are whole numbers); the result is `floor(1000000 p + 0.5)`, raised to 1 if smaller, and made to fall by one if equal to the rung before (both noted on the line; if it cannot fall: `TAIL_CANNOT_FALL`). The last line, `tailPpm 1000000,126000,...`, is the list to paste. Other codes: `TAIL_SAMPLE_FORMAT`, `TAIL_SAMPLE_DUPLICATE`, `TAIL_METRIC_MISSING`, `TAIL_OPTION`, `TAIL_LEVELS`, `TAIL_FIT_FAILED`.

**The boundary.** Floating point is allowed in exactly one place: `src/EngineCommon/EngineCommon.TailTool`, an offline authoring aid. The tool prints its inputs (the metric, the sample size, minimum and maximum, a SHA-256 of the sample, the percentile and threshold, the number of exceedances) and the fitted `xi` and `sigma` to six places, so a person can reproduce the integer by hand; the integers it writes are ordinary document data and the runtime never sees a float or the tool. A warning line is printed when `xi` is outside -0.5 to 0.5 (the range the estimator is validated for; a bounded or very heavy tail). Guards (`EngineCommon.Balance.Tests/SourceGuardTests`): the reducer and the three `*Cli.Balance.cs` files contain no float, clock or random source, and no file access except the one `File.ReadAllText` of the scenario (the shared Roslyn guard, shown to fail on a planted `double` and `DateTime`); no project references `EngineCommon.TailTool` except `EngineCommon.Balance.Tests`, the tool references nothing, and `EngineCommon.Balance` is referenced only by `*.Cli` and `*.Tests` projects (checked over every `.csproj` under `src`, with the rule itself shown to fail on synthetic project sets).

**What the tool cannot do here.** A hazard ladder's rungs are chosen by the designer; this engine has no continuous per-onset magnitude, so the sweep does not produce a sample of onset severities. The tool needs a magnitude sample you believe tracks severity (historical return-period data written as `sample` rows, or a sweep metric used as a stand-in, as below). It proposes a number; the person decides.

### 13.2 What was built

```
WorldEventsEngine.Cli simulate <world.json> <scenario.json> [<steps>] --rare <hazard>:<rung> [--effort N] [--replications R] [--from-seed S] [--days D] [--rows]
WorldEventsEngine.Cli simulate ... --seeds N [...] --onsets
```

`--rare quake:great` estimates the chance that, within the horizon, a **natural onset** of the hazard is drawn at that rung or worse. The rare event is cut into nested levels by the score of a run (highest drawn rung plus one of any natural onset so far): a ladder of R + 1 rungs gives R + 1 levels. One class owns each part. `EngineCommon.Balance.FixedEffortSplitting` is the sampler (engine-free, integers only, a pure function of root seed, effort and replications); `RareHazardModel` (Cli) is the engine side: it runs the scenario with the same private `Live` loop every command uses (which gained a start step, a first scripted call and an early stop), stops at the first step whose changes carry the level, and keeps the whole world as the checkpoint; the next stage calls the new `WorldRuntime.Branch(set)` on the checkpoint with the same document under a derived seed. `OriginRule` is the one rule for a natural onset: a `Started` change at full power (1,000 per mille) among the step's own changes, not those of scripted calls, because an onset that spread from a neighbour starts weaker and a script or card chooses its rung; a hazard that spreads without losing power (reach above 0 and falloff 1,000) is refused (`BAL_ONSET_AMBIGUOUS`) rather than guessed at.

The report: one `replication` line each with `--rows` (`hits h1/N,h2/N,... runs r estimate-ppb v`), then one `rare-estimate` line: runs used, `mean-ppb`, `se-ppb`, `band-ppb` (mean +- 2 se, cut at 0 and 10^9), `median-ppb`, `median95-ppb` (the distribution-free 95% interval, from six replications up), min, max and how many replications were `extinct` (a stage with no hit: estimate 0, counted, not hidden), then a note and a digest. All whole numbers; each replication is rounded down to a ppb and the standard error up, so the rounding is under 1 ppb a value. A stage that finds no checkpoint ends its replication; a checkpoint already above the next level reaches it without a run. New codes: `BAL_RARE_MISSING`, `BAL_RARE_TARGET` (unknown hazard or rung, or not `hazard:rung`; the message lists the choices), `BAL_EFFORT_RANGE` (1 to 5,000), `BAL_REPLICATIONS_RANGE` (1 to 1,000), `BAL_ONSET_AMBIGUOUS`; exit 2 as the sweep's.

`--onsets` adds to the sweep, per seed, `onset.hazard.<id>.natural`, `.open-at-end` and `.drawn-at-least.<rung>` (the `positive` column of the last is the plain Monte Carlo count that `--rare` estimates), `onset.outbreak.<id>.started` and `.open-at-end`; and **lists of values, one per onset** (`events <name> n .. seeds .. min p10 p50 p90 p99 max mean` and, with `--rows`, `onset <name> <seed> <value>`): per natural hazard onset `hazard.<id>.rung` (highest rung reached), `.steps` and `.size` (the sum over its steps of the engine's own `Intensity`, per mille); per outbreak (first case in a region until no one is exposed or infectious; a scripted seeding counts) `outbreak.<id>.dead`, `.peak`, `.steps`, `.outbroke`; and for both `.open`, 1 when it was still going at the horizon (its magnitudes are then a lower bound, and are in the list, because leaving them out would drop exactly the largest events; a horizon long enough to end most of them is the author's choice). `SeedSweep.RunWithEvents` and `SweepOptions.Parse(args, engineFlags)` are the shared parts; a sweep without `--onsets` prints exactly what it printed before.

The tail tool reads `onset <name> <seed> <value>` rows as well as `sample` rows (several per seed are allowed; a name with both kinds is refused, `TAIL_SAMPLE_AMBIGUOUS`) and prints `per-onset` on its first line. **The honest finding: splitting did not help here.** On the valley it uses about half the runs of plain Monte Carlo for a band about twice as wide: no better per run. On the 20 ppm world it is worse. The reason is structural: the rung of an onset is drawn **at the instant the onset happens**, so the checkpoint of level 1 already holds the answer to "is this onset a `great`?"; restarting it under other seeds cannot redraw it, and the share of stage-2 entries that succeed is a binomial count over the few distinct checkpoints (14 to 40), exactly the variance plain Monte Carlo has for that part. Restarts only help for what comes after the checkpoint (a second onset in a cluster; the valley's quake excites itself, which is why `quake` is not worse than plain). Splitting pays when a rare event is reached by accumulation through states that can be resumed (the gambler's ruin), not when it is decided by one draw at a jump. For this engine's rungs the useful tools are the plain sweep at scale (`--seeds` with `--onsets`: 40,000 seeds in 6 minutes) and the identity above; the sampler is kept for events that do build up (clusters, outbreak growth, stress chains) and is validated to agree with exact values. Making it efficient for a rung would need the severity redrawn at the checkpoint (a pre-draw state, not available because the onset trial and its severity share a step) or a conditional estimator that uses the ladder's known tails, which holds only where the rung does not change later onsets; neither is built. Different effort per stage (more runs at stage 1) is the cheap next step and is not built either.

### 14.4 What this is, honestly

It **beats plain Monte Carlo** here (3 to 19 times at equal engine steps), where pass 50's splitting did not. But the reason is not that levels are reached by accumulation. A rung is one draw; after the onset nothing is left to split on: the engine's intensity curve is a fixed ramp of the rung and the durations drawn with it (`Intensity` has no randomness), and escalation, the only random progression after an onset, is not part of the event. So the pre-draw restart works by **redrawing the one draw inside its known bound**. The stage fractions after the first estimate by simulation what the ladder states exactly (`Ladder[L].TailPpm / Ladder[L-1].TailPpm`, 12.6% and 12.7% on the valley; the measured fractions are a little higher because a later onset in the same run can also reach the rung), and the stage that carries the variance is the plain one, "does an onset happen in the year" (5.6% of runs on the valley). A conditional estimator that used the ladder's tails and a plain count of onsets would have **less** variance than this sampler. The sampler is the form that stays valid when the rest of the event depends on the simulation (a later onset in the same run), and it checks by simulation that the engine's draw honours the ladder; it is not the best tool for a rung alone. The cost is mostly stage 1: 94% of valley runs end without an onset after the full horizon. A different effort per stage helped there (1000 at stage 1, 300 later).

### 14.5 The tail tool's shape checks

The tool invented a tail on the bimodal, bounded plague data of 13.5. It now checks the shape before reading a level above the threshold from the fit (levels at or under the threshold are counted from the sample and are not checked, so the pass-50 numbers for them are unchanged).

- **Gaps (`ModalityCheck`, `TAIL_MULTIMODAL`).** The dip test and Silverman's test are the established ones (14.1). I used a simpler test that suits whole-number data with hundreds of ties and needs no null table: walk the sorted sample; at each step of 2 or more compare its width with the mean step of the 25 values on each side (the step itself left out, a mean under 1 counted as 1), looking only at steps with at least max(5, n/50) values on both sides (the thin end of a heavy tail has wide steps by nature); a ratio above 30 is a gap. **Calibration:** 2,700 simulated unimodal samples (exponential, Pareto 0.5 and 1, lognormal, normal, uniform; n = 200, 1,000, 5,000; 150 each) gave **0 false alarms**, and 200 of 200 two-cluster mixtures (800 values near 1,000 and 200 near 3,000) were found (a test, fixed seeds). The limit 30 was only set above the largest ratio seen, so it misses subtle shoulders: it finds clusters separated by a stretch many times the spacing. Decision: a gap with values above the threshold on both sides of it is **refused**; a gap with the threshold at its lower edge is a **warning** (the fit describes only the cluster above it); a gap wholly under the threshold belongs to the body and is not reported.
- **Bounds (`BoundCheck`, `TAIL_BOUNDED`).** The excesses are resampled 200 times (a fixed SplitMix64 sequence, so the same sample gives the same answer) and each resample is fitted by the same estimator; if at least 97.5% of the fits have a negative shape the tail is called bounded (the end the fit implies is printed). A bounded tail with a level above the largest value seen is **refused** (the sample holds nothing about it); with every level inside the sample it is a warning. 60 simulated heavy tails (Pareto 0.3) were never called bounded and 60 uniform samples always were (a test).
- **Pass-50 plague onsets, 1,500 steps, 1,831 outbreaks (levels 1000,1500,2000,2500).** Thresholds 50, 70, 80: `refused TAIL_MULTIMODAL` (no outbreak lies between 615 and 1,616 deaths; 1,631 at or under, 200 at or over; the step is 222 times the spacing around it). Thresholds 90 and 95: `refused TAIL_BOUNDED` (200 of 200 resampled fits negative, the fit ends at 1,926 to 1,952, the largest outbreak is 1,950). **The 2,000-death rung is declined at every threshold.** Levels 0,500,1000,1500 at 90%: `1000000,260513,109230,109229` as before (no level beyond the threshold, no check).

### 15.2 The estimator and why it is exact

A run is the plain scenario lived from a seed. Let c_t be the chance the engine itself uses for the target hazard's trial at opportunity t (it depends on the state at that moment: season, exposure, difficulty, the excitation left by earlier onsets; it is 0 when a condition, an exclusion or the director's pacing would veto the onset), and let T be the first opportunity at which an onset is drawn at the rung or worse (or the end of the horizon). The run's weight is **Z = q (c_1 + ... + c_T)**, the trial at T included. Then E[Z] = P(some natural onset reaches the rung within the horizon), exactly.

Reason (Wald's identity for the compensator of the onset process, stopped at its first qualifying point): at each opportunity the indicator "an onset arises and reaches the rung" has, given everything before the severity draw, the conditional mean c_t q, and whether the opportunity is open (no qualifying onset before it) is decided before it. So the sum of those indicators up to T, which is 1 when the event happens and 0 otherwise, has the mean of the sum of c_t q up to T. Nothing is assumed about how earlier draws change later chances: excitation (Hawkes), cooldown, the step after an onset having no trial, a lasting onset blocking the region, a condition that follows the scenario's facts are all inside c_t, because the engine reports the chance of the state it is in. A replication's estimate is the mean of Z over its runs in whole numbers (weights in units of 10^-12, rounded down to a ppb), through the shared `SplitSummary`, so the report is the `--rare` report with the words `estimator conditional` after the target; rows say `informative k/N` (runs that met any chance). A run costs at most the horizon and stops at the first event; `work-steps` is the engine steps lived.

**Decisions.** (1) Hawkes and other modifiers need no code of their own: the chance comes from `WorldRuntime.OnsetChance` through the watcher. (2) Horizon ending mid-onset: a trial in the last step counts, and what an onset does after the horizon does not matter, since the rung is drawn at the onset (tested: a one-step horizon weighs every run exactly c q; onsets lasting past the horizon give q (1 - (1 - c)^100)). (3) `when`, `excludes`, `paced`: **supported**, not refused (the pre-draw mode refuses them because a restart cannot reproduce a veto): the watcher reports a vetoed trial at chance 0 using the engine's own `VetoOf`. (4) A hazard that spreads at full power (`BAL_ONSET_AMBIGUOUS` in the other modes) is accepted: the watcher sees trials, not `Started` changes, so a spread onset is never taken for a natural one. (5) Event: an onset **drawn by a trial** at the rung or worse; it differs from `--rare` only for an onset a card effect starts at full power in the step's own changes, which `OriginRule` would count (no sample has one). (6) Forced steps (`BranchForcingOnset`) are not reported. (7) New code `BAL_CONDITIONAL_MIX` for `--conditional` with `--predraw` or `--efforts` (there are no stages); `--effort N` is the number of runs in a replication.

### 15.3 A finding on the way: the lab world's "exact" value was not quite the engine's

Passes 50 and 52 took 1 - (1 - c t)^365 as exact for the lab (one hazard that lasts one step). The engine gives a hazard no trial in the step after it ends (the removal happens at the start of the next step and the hazard is ready again the step after), so an onset blocks the next step's trial. The engine's exact value is a renewal chain (free, blocked), computed in BigInteger in the tests: 20 ppm `great` 116,790 ppb (not 116,793), 200 ppm 1,167,091 (1,167,320), 2,000 ppm `mid` 87,741,101 (87,887,180), `great` 11,589,623 (11,612,238). The difference is 0.0026% at 20 ppm and 0.2% at 2,000 ppm, under the sampling error of the earlier passes' estimates, so their conclusions stand; this estimator is compared with the engine's value. `WatchTests` shows the blocked step directly.

**Coverage, honestly.** The standard "at least 17 of 20 bands cover the exact lab value" is **met in three of the four lab cases (19, 20, 20) and not at 20 ppm (7 of 20)**. It is not bias (the mean of all 420 replications is within 2 ppb of the exact value). At 20 ppm an event happens in 0.012% of runs, so most estimates of 4,200 runs contain no stop and sit on one value (116,798) with a standard error of 1 ppb; the band mean +- 2 se is a normal approximation of a distribution with a point mass, and it excludes the exact value 8 ppb below the point mass. An estimator whose spread is smaller than the effect of one rare stop cannot have a normal band that covers; the mean over many estimates is right, the band of a single estimate is not to be trusted at that rate. For the valley the plain references are the noisy ones, so 0 of 20 is no test; the conditional estimates lie within 0.4 and 0.6 standard errors of the pooled plain values and within 1.3% and 0.3% of the pre-draw means.

**Efficiency, honestly.** In variance times engine steps the conditional estimator beats plain Monte Carlo by 2,600 (valley `quake`) to 23,000 (valley `great`) times and pass-52 `--predraw` by about 900 and 1,700 times. In the lab the ratios are meaningless (the estimator is nearly deterministic there: runs differ only by where a rare stop falls; the sd of one 200-run replication is 45 ppb at 116,790). The absolute numbers are the useful ones: a valley estimate to 0.4% (sd 3,716 of 988,435) from 2,100 runs (about 770,000 engine steps), where pre-draw needed 454,727 steps a replication for an sd of 51,342. It is exact for the event of 15.2 whatever the engine does between onsets, which splitting is not. It cannot do what is not decided at a trial: a rare event that builds up after an onset (outbreak growth, a stress chain, escalation) has no per-trial draw to condition on, and the splitting modes of sections 13 and 14 remain the tools for those.
